Vango is a loyalty card inside a Nimiq Pay wallet. A customer pays the shop in NIM, the
payment carries a short memo like `vango:5FQQ2J56`, and that payment on the Nimiq chain is
the stamp. There is no plastic, no app to install, and no fee on the payment.

## Why it works this way

A paper card gets lost and a plastic one needs a terminal, a printer and a monthly bill.
Every loyalty app asks the customer to install something, and most of them ask the shop to
trust a company with its money.

Vango asks for neither. The customer is already paying, so the payment does the work of the
stamp. The NIM goes straight from the customer's wallet to the shop's own wallet, in about a
second, and Vango never touches it. What Vango does is read the chain back and count.

Because the count lives on a public chain, both sides can check it. A customer can prove a
visit even if Vango disappears tomorrow, and a shop cannot quietly reset anyone's card.

## One visit, end to end

1. A merchant opens a card: the reward, the rule, and the wallet the money goes to.
2. A customer opens the card from a link, a QR on the counter, or the eight-character code.
3. The customer pays in Nimiq Pay. The memo is filled in for them.
4. The server fetches that transaction from a Nimiq node and, if it satisfies the card's
   rule, it becomes one stamp. A chain watcher makes the same pass in the background.
5. When the card is full, the customer signs a message. That signature becomes a six-digit
   code and a QR the merchant scans at the counter.
