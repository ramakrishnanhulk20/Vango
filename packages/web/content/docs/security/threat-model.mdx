---
title: Threat model
description: Who attacks Vango, where they can get in, what stops each attack, and the proof.
---

Vango never holds money. Payments go from a customer's Nimiq Pay wallet straight to the
merchant's address, and cashback goes from the merchant's wallet straight to the
customer's. The server only reads the chain and keeps score. That removes the biggest
class of attacks (nobody can drain a vault that does not exist) and leaves the ones below.
Every claim here has a script that tries to break it, run against the live testnet, with
the output saved under `docs/proofs/`.

## Who attacks, and what they want

- **A customer who wants free rewards.** Fake a stamp, count one payment twice, redeem a
  card that is not full, redeem the same reward twice, use somebody else's reward.
- **A merchant who wants to look popular or avoid paying.** Stamp their own card to inflate
  numbers, mark cashback as paid without paying, confirm a reward that was never earned.
- **A stranger.** Take over a session, read or confirm another merchant's rewards, guess a
  six-digit code, flood the API.
- **A bug.** Two processes stamping the same payment, a retried request paying twice, a
  node that drops a request mid-read.

## Entry points

1. The mini app's JSON API (login, cards, stamp claim, wallet, redemption).
2. The chain watcher reading public RPC.
3. The public RPC node itself (shared, rate limited, could lie or lag).
4. The Nimiq Pay WebView (the app's own code runs there; the wallet is outside it).

## What stops each attack

| Attack | What stops it | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| Fake a stamp without paying | A stamp is only ever written from a confirmed on-chain transaction the server fetched itself, to the card's receiving address, with the card's memo, at or above the minimum | unit tests in `stamps.test.ts`; live prove-it steps 4 to 6 |
| Count one payment twice (retry, watcher and phone both seeing it, replayed claim) | Unique index on the transaction hash; insert is "do nothing on conflict" | "refuses the same transaction hash twice"; prove-it step 5 |
| Merchant stamps their own card | A card always receives at the merchant's own wallet address, and a sender equal to the merchant's visible address, remote account, or the receiving address of any of their cards is refused. Nimiq itself rejects a payment whose sender is its recipient | "refuses a payment from the merchant's own address", "a merchant cannot stamp one of their cards from another of their cards' address"; prove-it step 4 |
| Pay a tiny amount for a stamp | Per-card minimum, default 1 NIM | "refuses a payment below the card minimum"; prove-it step 6 |
| Log in as someone else | Ed25519 signature over a single-use, ten-minute challenge; the public key must derive to the claimed address | `auth.test.ts`; prove-it step 7 replay |
| Replay a login or redemption signature | Nonce is single use and expires | "refuses a reused login nonce", "refuses a replayed redemption signature" |
| Redeem before the card is full | Server recomputes progress from stamps minus confirmed redemptions at start, sign and confirm | "refuses to redeem a card that is not full"; prove-it step 8 |
| Redeem twice | A partial unique index allows one pending reward per card and customer, so two concurrent signs cannot both open one; confirm is a single conditional update; consumed stamps are subtracted | "two concurrent signs for one full card open exactly one reward", property test "stamps consumed never exceed stamps earned" |
| Read or confirm another merchant's reward | Every redemption lookup is joined to the caller's own cards | "refuses a merchant confirming another merchant's redemption"; prove-it step 9 |
| Guess a six-digit code | Codes only resolve among one merchant's live pending rewards (ten-minute window), are unique among them by a partial unique index, and are rate limited by IP; the merchant is the only caller who can use them | `redeem.test.ts`; "six-digit codes never collide among one merchant's pending rewards"; rate-limit test |
| Mark cashback paid without paying | The server fetches the hash from the chain and requires: included, recipient is the customer, value at least the amount owed, sender is the merchant; one hash settles at most one reward | "cashback settles once and the same hash cannot settle twice", "refuses a cashback payment that did not come from the merchant"; prove-it step 10 |
| Steal a session | Tokens are 32 random bytes with a `vs1.` prefix, stored only as a hash, expire in 30 days, never logged; a token is never a record id | `auth.test.ts` |
| Flood the API | Rate limits keyed by IP (60 per minute, tighter on auth and redeem); every hit logged | "rate limits repeated login attempts" |
| RPC node lies or lags | Stamps need `blockNumber > 0`; the watcher cursor only moves over blocks whose payments were handled; the public node is one of several that can be configured | `watcher.test.ts` |
| Busy address outruns the watcher | Paged reads of 500 with a cap and a next-pass catch-up | "reads a busy address across more than one page" |

## Weaknesses we did not fix, stated plainly

- **A merchant can stamp customers who did not really buy anything** by paying them and
  asking them to pay back with the memo. That costs the merchant real NIM per stamp and
  gains them nothing but a vanity number. Not worth defending.
- **A customer with two wallets is two customers.** Stamps follow the wallet. That is how
  physical stamp cards work too.
- **The public RPC node is a trusted reader.** If it served false transactions, stamps
  could be wrong. Mitigation available later: read from two independent nodes and require
  agreement. Not built in v1.
- **Session tokens have no logout.** A stolen token is valid until it expires. A logout
  route is a small addition planned for v1.1.
- **No merchant identity beyond a wallet.** Anyone can create a card called "Starbucks".
  Customers see the merchant's address and the card's name; brand verification is not a
  v1 feature and is stated as such in the app. What a card cannot do is point at
  somebody else's wallet: it always receives at the wallet that created it.
- **Six-digit codes are guessable in principle.** They are useless without being that
  merchant's signed-in phone, within ten minutes, under a rate limit. Stated, accepted.

## Data we hold, and why

Wallet addresses (public by nature), card definitions, stamp records (which are public
on the chain anyway), redemption records, session hashes, the user's chosen language and
currency. No names, no emails, no device identifiers in v1. Nothing that is not either
public on the chain or needed to keep score.

## Reading this alongside the proofs

Every "prove-it step" in the table above is a numbered step in
[the prove-it command](/docs/developers/the-prove-it-command), run against the live testnet with
its output saved. Every named test is in the offline suite under `packages/server/src`. What was
checked and what was not is set out in [audits](/docs/security/audits).
