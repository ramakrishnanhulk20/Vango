---
title: Trust model
description: The chain is the ledger, the server keeps score, and Vango never holds money.
---

Vango is built so that the parts you have to trust are as small as possible, and so that the
parts holding money are none.

## The chain is the ledger

A stamp is not a row somebody typed. It is a confirmed Nimiq payment, public and permanent,
that anybody can look up on an explorer by its hash. The card's rule, the memo and the
receiving address are all visible before the customer pays. If the Vango server vanished
tomorrow, every stamp would still be on the chain and could be counted again from scratch.

## The server indexes and keeps score

The server's whole job is reading. It fetches transactions from a Nimiq node, decides which
of them satisfy a card's rule, and keeps the count. It holds no balance, has no wallet key,
and cannot move a single NIM. Money moves twice in Vango, and both times it is a wallet
paying another wallet directly: the customer paying the merchant, and the merchant paying
cashback back to the customer.

## What counts as a stamp

All six of these have to be true, and the server checks each one against the chain, not
against what the phone claims:

1. The transaction is **included** in a block.
2. It was **paid to the card's receiving address**, which is always the merchant's own wallet.
3. It carries the card's **memo**, `vango:CODE`.
4. Its value is **at or above the card's minimum**, 1 NIM by default.
5. The **sender is not the merchant**, nor any address the merchant controls.
6. That **transaction hash has never been counted before**, enforced by a unique index in the
   database, so a retry, the watcher and the phone all seeing the same payment still produce
   exactly one stamp.

## What counts as a redemption

The customer signs a message naming the card, valid once and for ten minutes. The server
recomputes progress from stamps minus rewards already taken, and only then opens a reward.
The reward becomes a token, plus a six-digit code the merchant can type. Only the merchant
who owns the card can read or confirm it. One card and customer can have one pending reward
at a time, and confirming is a single conditional write, so two taps cannot hand over two
rewards.

On a cashback card, "confirmed" is not the end. The merchant pays from their own wallet, and
the server checks on chain that the payment was included, went to the customer, was at least
the amount owed, and came from the merchant. One payment settles at most one reward.

## What Vango never does

- It never holds, escrows or forwards your money.
- It never asks for a key, a seed phrase or a password.
- It never lets a card point at an address other than the wallet that created it.
- It never writes a stamp from anything except a transaction it fetched from the chain itself.
- It never stores your name, email or device identifier.
